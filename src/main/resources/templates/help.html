Help and common questions

Plain-English answers about filing a claim, checking in each week, and getting paid.

Before you file

Who can get unemployment benefits?

Usually, people who lost their job through no fault of their own. That includes being laid off, your employer closing, or being let go for reasons that weren't misconduct.

If you quit for a personal reason, or were fired for breaking a rule on purpose, you usually don't qualify. You can still file. A caseworker reviews every claim, and you can appeal a denial.

How is my weekly benefit worked out?

Your weekly benefit is 50% of your average weekly pay, but never less than or more than a week.

Example: you earned $800 a week. Half of that is $400, so your weekly benefit is $400. If you earned $1,400 a week, half would be $700, but the most you can get is .

What do I need to file?
  • Your last employer's name
  • The last day you worked
  • Why your job ended
  • Your average weekly pay before taxes (a pay stub helps)
  • An email address you check

After you file

What happens after I file?

You get a confirmation number right away. A caseworker then reviews your claim. They may contact your employer to confirm why your job ended. You can follow along on the Check my claim page.

What documents should I upload?

Anything that backs up what you told us. Good examples are a layoff or separation letter, your last pay stub, or a photo ID. Files must be PDF, PNG, or JPEG and up to 5 MB each.

What if my claim is denied?

You'll see the reason on your claim page. If you think it's wrong, you can file an appeal within 30 days of the decision. Explain what happened with facts and dates, and upload any proof. A supervisor who did not make the first decision will look at your claim again. You can appeal once.

Getting paid each week

What is a weekly check-in?

Once your claim is approved, you answer a few questions for each week (Sunday to Saturday) to get paid for that week. You can check in for a week after it ends.

You'll be asked if you were able to work, if you turned down a job, how many employers you contacted, and how much you earned.

What counts as a job contact?

You need at least 3 each week. Each of these counts as one:

  • Applying for a job online or in person
  • Sending your resume to an employer
  • Going to a job interview
  • Going to a job fair and talking to employers

Keep a simple list of who you contacted and when, in case you're asked.

Can I work part-time and still get paid?

Yes. Tell us what you earned that week, before taxes. The first 25% of your weekly benefit in earnings is ignored. After that, each dollar you earn lowers your payment by a dollar.

Example: your weekly benefit is $400, so the first $100 you earn is ignored. If you earn $160, your payment drops by $60, to $340. You keep your $160 too, so taking the work always leaves you with more money.

Why wasn't I paid for my first week?

Your first week that qualifies is an unpaid "waiting week". Many states have this rule. You still need to check in for it. Every week after that is paid if you qualify.

How long can I get benefits?

Up to 26 weeks of full payments per claim. Your claim page shows how much is left.
